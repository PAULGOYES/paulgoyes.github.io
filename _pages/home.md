Geophysics combines physics, mathematics, and computational methods to study the Earth's subsurface using indirect measurements such as seismic, gravimetric, and electromagnetic data.
It plays a key role in energy exploration, natural hazard assessment, and environmental monitoring, where direct observation of the subsurface is not possible.
Inverse theory and deep learning have become essential tools in this field, allowing researchers  to accurately reconstruct the subsurface from incomplete or noisy data.

### About me

I am a geophysicist working at the intersection of inverse theory, signal processing, and deep learning applied to geophysical exploration. I hold a Bachelor’s degree in Geology from Universidad Industrial de Santander (UIS), a Master’s degree in Geology/Geophysics from Perm State University (Russia), and a PhD in Computer Science at UIS (2026), where my research focused on optimizing 3D survey geometries and recovery algorithms for compressive seismic acquisition.
My work has been published in journals including IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing, IEEE Geoscience and Remote Sensing Letters, Leading Edge, and Gondwana Research. In 2023, I completed a research internship at the Computational Imaging Group at Washington University in St. Louis, focused on diffusion models for inverse problems. I am currently a researcher working on multi-physics inversion for geothermal exploration and deep learning applications in geophysics.
